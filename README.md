# CastValue — Quantifying the EUR/MW Value of DAA Price Forecasts for BESS

**CastValue** is an open backtesting framework that measures what a Day-Ahead Auction (DAA) price forecast is actually worth — in euros per megawatt of BESS capacity, per year.

Most forecast providers sell on MAE. Most traders buy on MAE. CastValue shows why that is the wrong metric, and replaces it with one that directly reflects trading P&L.

---

## The Problem

A forecast with a low MAE can destroy more trading value than one with a high MAE. This is not a theoretical edge case — it happens regularly, and this repository demonstrates it empirically on real German DAA price data.

The reason: what matters for BESS dispatch is not *how close* the forecast price is to the actual price, but *whether the forecast correctly ranks the cheap and expensive hours of the day*. A forecast can be numerically accurate yet systematically wrong about timing — causing the optimizer to charge when it should discharge, and vice versa.

---

## What CastValue Measures

CastValue is defined as the annual DAA trading margin generated by optimizing BESS dispatch on a given forecast, minus the margin generated by a naive **persistence baseline** (yesterday's prices as today's forecast):

```
CastValue(k) = AnnualMargin(forecast k) − AnnualMargin(persistence)
```

A positive CastValue means the forecast adds real EUR/MW above what any trader could achieve with zero forecasting effort. A negative CastValue means the forecast is worse than doing nothing.

---

## Why This Matters

**For BESS traders and asset managers:**
Stop evaluating forecast providers on MAE. CastValue tells you directly how much each forecast contributes to your annual trading margin, in the currency that appears on your P&L. Use this tool to compare your current provider against alternatives — and against the theoretical ceiling of perfect foresight.

**For forecast providers:**
Benchmark and showcase your forecast in the metric your clients care about. A CastValue number is a commercially credible claim: it tells potential customers exactly how many EUR/MW per year your forecast is worth, backed by a transparent, reproducible methodology.

---

## The MAE Paradox

The backtester surfaces a systematic finding we call the **MAE Paradox**: days with high forecast error (high MAE) do not reliably lead to high profit loss, and days with low forecast error do not reliably lead to high profit capture.

We classify days into two categories:
- **Bad Forecast, Good Capture** — high MAE, but the optimizer still dispatched at the right times
- **Good Forecast, Bad Capture** — low MAE, but a subtle timing shift caused the optimizer to miss the most profitable spreads

This has direct implications for how forecast quality should be specified in commercial contracts.

---

## Methodology

The backtester runs a linear programme (LP) for each day of the dataset, optimising BESS charge/discharge decisions to maximise expected DAA revenue, subject to energy capacity, power capacity, and cycle constraints. Three scenarios are evaluated for each day:

| Scenario | Forecast used |
|---|---|
| Persistence | Yesterday's actual prices |
| Electricity Maps | Electricity Maps DAA price forecast (benchmarked provider) |
| Perfect Foresight | Actual prices (theoretical ceiling) |

Trading decisions are optimised on forecast prices. Realised margin is always calculated on **actual prices**.

The dataset covers the German/Luxembourg DAA market (EPEX SPOT DE-LU) across 2024–2025, including the October 2025 market structure change from hourly to quarter-hourly DAA products.

For the full mathematical formulation see `DAA_DE_LU_Formulation.pdf`.

## How to Benchmark Your Own Forecast

To run CastValue on your own forecast data, prepare a CSV with the following columns:

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| `timestamp_utc` | Quarter-hour timestamps in UTC |
| `daa_actuals` | Actual DAA settlement prices [EUR/MWh] |
| `daa_persistence_forecast` | Persistence forecast (previous day's actuals) [EUR/MWh] |
| `daa_provider_forecast` | Your forecast [EUR/MWh] |

Replace the `daa_provider_forecast` column in `master_energy_data_v1.csv` with your own data, update the scenario name in the notebook, and run all cells.
